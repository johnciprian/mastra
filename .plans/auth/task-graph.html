The task graph, and what it revealed

Decomposing the four-phase plan into 65 dependency-linked tasks produced one uncomfortable finding and one useful one. The uncomfortable finding is that my earlier estimate was wrong. The useful one is that the long pole is documentation, not the backend rewrite.

65 tasks 54.5 person-days 16.5 days critical path 7 hard gates acyclic, single root

Correcting the estimate I gave you

Last turn I said “~3.5–4 weeks, one engineer.” That conflated two different numbers, and the decomposition makes the difference concrete.

16.5 days is the critical path — the floor, achievable only with enough people to run every lane in parallel. That is where my 3.5-week figure actually came from. 54.5 person-days is the total effort, which for one engineer is closer to eleven weeks, not four. Plan against 54.5; use 16.5 only to know that adding a fourth engineer buys you nothing.

The shape of the dependency graph

Everything funnels through one boundary proof and one contract file, then fans into four lanes that rejoin at the re-grade. The accented path is the longest chain; the two lanes drawn with slack can start late without moving the end date.

Scheduling

Three honest readings of the same graph.

One engineer
~11 weeks

54.5 person-days end to end. The graph shape barely matters; you are effort-bound, not dependency-bound. Ship lane by lane and take the gates seriously — they are your only regression insurance.

Two engineers
~6 weeks

Best value. Split at K7: one takes identity, fakes and conformance (K8–K10, K17, K18), the other takes state, session and the descriptor (K11–K14). They rejoin at K17, then split again across backend and UI+docs.

Three or more
~4 weeks

Approaches the 16.5-day floor plus review overhead. A fourth engineer buys nothing — the kit spine is inherently serial, and D5 needs a reader who was not involved in writing any of it.

Things the graph makes visible that the plan did not
  • Docs is the long pole, not the rewrite. Lane D is only 6 tasks and 8.5 days, but it sits at the end of the longest chain and its gate needs a person who was not involved. Start it the moment K18 lands; do not treat it as the thing you do after the code works.
  • Four tasks are deliberately not on the path to A−. B18, B19, U9 and R3 are the one-release-later deletions. They are in the graph so they are not forgotten, but blocking on them would be a scheduling error.
  • K6 is cheap and disproportionately valuable. Half a day, and it converts your hard licence requirement from a code-review habit into a build error. Nothing else in the graph is that leveraged.
  • Lane B has one uncomfortable cluster. B8, B9, B10 and B11 change state, cookies, logout and refresh together, and all four are what a live sign-in depends on. That is exactly what B2's flag exists for — do not skip it because the tests are green.
  • C5 may end in “no.” Adapting Supabase and Firebase is real work, and deciding not to ship one of them is a legitimate outcome. The task is written to accept a documented unsupported marker as done — that is honest, and it keeps a half-working provider out of your matrix.
